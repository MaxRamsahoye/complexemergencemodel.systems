Generative Dynamics Decomposed

Schmachtenberger’s generator functions read through the Complex Emergence Model: how each is co-produced across the structures and scales of human agency. These generator functions belong to the metastructure and macrostructure: not located in any one structure, but recurring system dynamics that emerge from and operate through all of them.

StructureThe Multi-Polar TrapExponential Technological AccelerationNarrow-Boundary Problem-Solving
Competing actors each do what, if all do it, leaves all worse off – and none can refrain aloneTechnological power grows exponentially, outpacing the wisdom and governance to wield itProblems solved in narrow, isolated ways, pushing harm elsewhere and creating new problems
Microstructure Individual AgencyIndividually reasonable choices: ‘if I don’t, someone else will’; responsibility diffusedUnprecedented leverage: a researcher, hacker or small team now wields capacities once reserved for statesSpecialists rewarded for narrow objectives, rarely placed to see second-order effects
Mesostructure Collective AgencyFirms and states as the principal players – yet coalitions, treaties and standards can escape the trapLabs, firms and defence establishments favour speed over safety; regulators lag behindOrganisations siloed by design, solving their problem by exporting its costs
Macrostructure Historical ProcessA selection process: cooperators are outcompeted, ratcheting rivalry into institutionsAcceleration as history’s long-run signature; the gap with institutional change wideningLayered fixes accumulate into complexity, with diminishing returns (Tainter)
Infrastructure Tech StackRaises the stakes and speed of rivalry – but verification and transparency tools can enforce cooperationCompounding compute, near-free replication and AI accelerating research itselfTechnologies built for specified functions, their side effects outside the brief; tech fixing tech
Interstructure Political EconomyMarkets, sovereign states and growth imperatives write the payoff matrix; unpriced externalities make defection cheapCapital, intellectual property and state investment reward acceleration; regulation slow and capturedMarkets price only what is traded; single metrics – GDP, shareholder value – institutionalise the isolated view
Superstructure Cultural PsychologyDefines what counts as winning and supplies us-and-them narratives – or shared norms that underwrite cooperation (Ostrom)Narratives of progress and disruption legitimise speed; sense-making lagsThe analytic paradigm of modernity: problems broken into parts, each to an expert
Substructure Evolutionary PsychologyStatus competition, in-group loyalty, short-term discounting – alongside reciprocity and norm enforcementEvolved for local, linear worlds: we intuit exponential growth and distant risks poorlyCognition tuned to immediate, local threats; a preference for simple causes and single culprits
