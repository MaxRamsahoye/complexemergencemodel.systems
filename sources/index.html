  2. Daniel Schmachtenberger

    The Emergence Model

    Schmachtenberger describes a society as three interacting layers: its infrastructure, the technologies and material systems through which it meets its needs; its social structure, the institutions of politics, law and economics; and its superstructure, its shared values, beliefs and culture. A change in any layer pressures the others, but the deepest and most durable shifts begin in the infrastructure. The model gives this project its way of tracing how a new technology stack reshapes political economy and cultural psychology.

    • Ken Wilber

      Integral Theory

      Wilber’s four-quadrant map holds that any whole has interior and exterior, individual and collective dimensions – experience, behaviour, culture and systems – and that none can be reduced to the others. It underlies the Emergence Model’s insistence that technological, institutional and cultural change be read together.

    • Marvin Harris

      Cultural Materialism: The Struggle for a Science of Culture

      1979

      Harris divides every society into infrastructure, structure and superstructure, and argues that the material conditions of production and reproduction are the primary drivers of cultural change. It is the source of the Emergence Model’s three layers, and of the priority it gives to infrastructure.

  3. Karl Marx

    Preface to A Contribution to the Critique of Political Economy

    1859

    In a few pages Marx sets out the materialist conception of history: the relations of production make up the economic structure of society, “the real foundation, on which arises a legal and political superstructure and to which correspond definite forms of social consciousness”. The base-superstructure model is the first of the layered models of civilisation this project traces.

  4. Structure, Agency & Conditioning

  5. B. F. Skinner

    Beyond Freedom and Dignity

    1971

    Skinner argues that behaviour is shaped by its consequences – by the contingencies of reinforcement an environment arranges – and that the ideal of an autonomous inner agent, defended in the name of freedom and dignity, hides this control rather than escaping it. Since every culture conditions its members, he asks that it be done by deliberate design rather than by accident.

    Source ↗

  6. Anthony Giddens

    The Constitution of Society: Outline of the Theory of Structuration

    1984

    Giddens refuses the choice between structure and agency. Social structures are both the medium and the outcome of the practices they organise – the “duality of structure” – so that agents reproduce, and can transform, the conditions that enable and constrain them.
